# Fusion Digest — September 2026

_Run: 2026-10-01_

A thin month for press coverage: the news feeds (World Nuclear News, Phys.org, ITER Newsline, EUROfusion) were dominated by economic and governance items, which the editorial filter drops. The results below all come from primary arXiv preprints with measured outcomes. They are preprints, not yet peer reviewed, and the summaries rest on the abstracts only.

## 1. First experimental evidence of helicon current drive on DIII-D
DIII-D ran dedicated experiments with a MW-level helicon system and reports the first definitive observation of auxiliary current drive by helicon waves on any device. The waves were launched through a traveling-wave antenna and deposited power in the plasma core. The measured electron-temperature response to helicon power agrees with time-dependent integrated modelling that combines ray tracing with thermal transport. What differs from earlier work is that the helicon was run as a real, continuous co-Ip current-drive source rather than only being modelled. In those shots the reconstructed safety-factor profile flattens significantly faster, and sawteeth are triggered earlier, than in comparison shots where the helicon was replaced by a similar amount of electron cyclotron heating. That contrast is the evidence that the effect is driven current and not just heating. The calculated helicon-driven current profile is peaked in the core, consistent with the observed power-deposition profile and with ray-tracing predictions. The abstract gives no current amplitude or efficiency figure, so a numerical delta against existing current-drive systems (ECCD, neutral beams, lower hybrid) cannot be stated from this source. For the technology, helicon current drive is proposed as a way to sustain steady-state tokamak operation in reactor conditions. A first experimental validation of the ray-tracing picture therefore moves the concept from theory toward a credible option for continuous operation.

**Framing.** Only one source, a US-led primary paper, so there is no cross-regional framing to compare. The paper itself frames the result around reactor relevance (steady-state operation in reactor conditions), which fits the US emphasis on pilot-plant pathways. No news outlet had covered it in this window.

**Sources.**
- [First Experimental Evidence of Helicon Current Drive](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12474) — arXiv (DIII-D)

## 2. W7-X: larger pellets give higher quasi-steady-state stored energy
Wendelstein 7-X uses a continuous cryogenic-pellet injector to produce long-pulse, high-performance plasmas. Hydrogen ice pellets deposit particles deep in the confined region, creating a density gradient that suppresses ion-temperature-gradient turbulence and improves confinement. After many pellets, density and temperature rise and performance begins to saturate. The authors analysed pellet-injection experiments from 2024 and 2025 in which pellet sizes varied unintentionally. This gave them a natural experiment, which shows a positive correlation between pellet size and quasi-steady-state stored energy. The trend is qualitatively what pellet-ablation physics predicts. Quantitatively, however, the measured deposition position differs from the neutral-gas-shielding (NGS) model, which suggests significant inward transport of the pellet cloud. That is a modelling gap that matters for designing future pellet systems. The paper concludes that injecting even larger pellets could improve performance further. Relative to the baseline in Appendix A (the 43 s triple-product record of May 2025, with about 90 pellets from the ORNL injector), this does not set a new record. It identifies a lever for improving on that regime. For the technology, it points to a concrete, hardware-level route to better steady-state stellarator performance, though with a saturation limit not yet understood.

**Framing.** A single European primary source, framed in the methodical-validation mode: what the data say about a physics model (NGS) and where it fails, rather than a record claim. No competing national framing is available for this item.

**Sources.**
- [Pellet-Size Scaling of Quasi-Steady-State Plasma Performance in Wendelstein 7-X](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17751) — arXiv (IPP / W7-X)

## 3. KSTAR: error-field identification in a single H-mode discharge
Error fields, small asymmetries in the magnetic field, can trigger disruptions, so they have to be measured and corrected. The "compass scan" method does this non-disruptively via island healing. Earlier demonstrations on DIII-D, JET and MAST-U worked only in Ohmic and L-mode plasmas, needed several discharges, and relied on strong real-time n=1 magnetics close to the plasma that are unlikely to be viable in future devices. KSTAR has now demonstrated the method in a single H-mode discharge. The change is a new set of triggering algorithms, built for fusion-pilot-plant operation, in the KSTAR plasma control system. KSTAR's strong RMP coils and long pulse lengths made it possible. The headline delta is a reduction from four discharges to one, up to 75% less operational time for a complete compass scan. The authors call it a significant step toward a disruption-free method for disruption-averse devices including ITER and fusion pilot plants. They also state that gaps remain before the method is viable and discuss how to close them. The result matters for machine availability: every discharge spared is time not lost on a device that cannot afford disruptions.

**Framing.** One Asian (Korean) primary source. It is framed explicitly around ITER and pilot-plant deployment, consistent with KFE's pattern of emphasising KSTAR's long-pulse capability and its ITER role. The comparison baseline is the group's own prior multi-discharge method on DIII-D, JET and MAST-U.

**Sources.**
- [Demonstration of H-mode Error Field Identification in a Single Discharge via Island Healing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30452) — arXiv (KSTAR)

## 4. Broadband lasers reduce cross-beam energy transfer losses in direct-drive ICF
In direct-drive inertial fusion, cross-beam energy transfer (CBET) redirects a large share of the incident laser energy out of the plasma. At the low-coherence Kunwu laser facility, two crossed beams with 0.6% bandwidth (up to 550 J, about 2.6×10^14 W/cm²) were used to test whether bandwidth helps. With two orthogonal beams in symmetric incidence, the total fractional scattered energy fell from 7.57% to 4.64%. In an asymmetric geometry that separates stimulated Brillouin scattering (SBS) from specular reflection, the SBS fraction fell from 3.75% to 0.46%, and specular reflection from about 2.2% to 1.3%. A coupled ray-tracing model explains this, since CBET amplification of narrowband reflected light is much weaker under broadband illumination. This is an experimental validation of a mechanism that had been predicted. A companion simulation preprint addresses a related instability, absolute two-plasmon decay, which preheats targets. By gradient descent through a differentiable wave solver, it finds that joint amplitude–phase optimisation raises the threshold to 5.6 I_mono at a peak-to-average ratio of 3.6. That compares with 4.5 I_mono at ratio 16 for the best transform-limited result, and 2.8 I_mono for the random-phase median. It is a simulation result with no experiment yet, and is included as context only. Together, they suggest broadband drivers could cut energy losses and preheat risk in direct drive, which bears on achievable gain.

**Framing.** Both sources are primary preprints. The Kunwu experiment is Chinese, the optimisation paper is framed against OMEGA-scale and ignition-scale conditions (US reference facilities), so the comparison baseline for the physics is US direct-drive work. No outlet coverage allows a genuine comparison of national framing, so none is manufactured. Note the two papers differ in kind: an experiment (small-scale, 550 J) and a simulation.

**Sources.**
- [Experimental demonstration of broadband-laser suppression of cross-beam energy transfer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36528) — arXiv (Kunwu facility)
- [Joint Amplitude-Phase Optimization of Broadband Lasers Raises Absolute Two-Plasmon-Decay Thresholds Above Coherence-Time Predictions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31942) — arXiv (simulation)

## 5. A continuous, 10 Hz laser-driven alpha source from proton–boron-11 reactions
Laser-driven proton–boron fusion is a compact way to produce alpha particles, but solid targets degrade and cannot be fired often. This work replaces the solid target with a continuously replenished ultrathin water-leaf jet that accelerates protons by target-normal sheath acceleration. The proton beam hits a downstream boron nitride converter and drives ¹¹B(p,α)⁸Be, which yields three alpha particles. Measurements were repeated over 18,000 consecutive shots at 10 Hz. Alpha yield and spectral fluence were measured and benchmarked against particle-transport simulations. Both simulation and experiment show a pronounced flattening of the alpha energy spectrum leaving the converter, caused by depth-dependent reaction yield and stopping power. The change relative to earlier solid-target work is the repetition capability and stability over many shots. The abstract gives no absolute yield figure, so no numerical comparison with earlier records is possible here. This is accelerator-driven (beam-on-converter) fusion, not a path to net energy, and the authors name targeted particle therapy and laser-driven plasma-fusion research as applications. Its significance for fusion energy is therefore modest, and it is included as a borderline item for its measured, sustained-operation result.

**Framing.** A single primary source, with no outlet coverage. It frames the result around applications and repetition-rate engineering, not an energy-gain claim. The caution is that "fusion" here means a beam-driven reaction, so any commercialization framing would overstate it.

**Sources.**
- [Continuous Laser-Driven α Source](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33483) — arXiv
